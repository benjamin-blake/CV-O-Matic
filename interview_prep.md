# Interview Playbook

Living notes for interview preparation. Read this before prepping for any
interview, drill the story bank out loud, and run the pre-interview checklist.
`/log-interview` appends to the Feedback log after every outcome, and
`/prep-interview` reads this file, so keep the section headings as they are.

## The pattern the feedback is pointing at

_Fill this in once real feedback arrives (`/log-interview` helps)._ Look for
the recurring theme across outcomes rather than reacting to any single one.

A very common pattern at senior level, worth checking yourself against from
the start: behavioural answers default to **approach and philosophy** ("here
is how I think about X") instead of a **specific story with a decision and a
result** ("here is a time I did X, the call I made, and what happened"). When
the concrete story never lands, the evidence of leadership, influence and
impact never lands either, so a panel cannot see the seniority even when it is
there. The raw material is usually already in `cv/cv_achievements.yaml`: this
is a retrieval-and-framing problem, and it is very trainable.

## The three habits to change

1. **Lead with the example, not the approach.** Open a behavioural answer with
   the specific situation in one or two sentences, then the action, then the
   result. Earn the method by showing the instance.
2. **Answer the decision, not the process.** When asked how you prioritise or
   what you would deprioritise, name the actual call you made and the trade-off
   behind it (chose X over Y because Z), not how you communicate during change.
3. **Always land the outcome.** End every story on a concrete result and what
   changed because of you. Memorise one number per story: a metric, an
   adoption, a promotion, an incident averted.

## STAR-R template

For each prepared story, write five short bullets and rehearse out loud:

- **S**ituation: one line of context.
- **T**ask: what was at stake and why it mattered (the "real consequences").
- **A**ction: what **you personally** did, including the decision and the trade-off.
- **R**esult: the outcome, with a number.
- **R**eflection: what you learned or would do again (signals seniority and self-awareness).

## Human APIs (the seniority reframe)

Good stakeholder management at senior and lead level is not extroversion: it
is building predictable, unambiguous interfaces between yourself and other
people, the same way you would design an interface between systems. Prepare
one fully worked STAR-R story for each of these archetypes. Lead with the
instance, never the method.

### 1. Scope creep / "what would you deprioritise" -> capacity as a visible trade-off

_Your story here: a time you made a trade-off explicit instead of silently
absorbing extra work or flatly refusing it. Land: the deadline kept, and what
was sequenced rather than dropped. Source ids: ..._

### 2. Technical disagreement -> a written decision with clear criteria

_Your story here: a time you resolved a disagreement by writing the options,
costs and the criteria under which the decision would be revisited. Land: the
consensus reached and what it unlocked. Source ids: ..._

### 3. Managing trust -> failure modes that tell people whose court the ball is in

_Your story here: a time you made status, ownership or risk visible so
stakeholders always knew what was happening and who was acting. Land: the
incident avoided or the trust gained. Source ids: ..._

### 4. "Why are you looking to move?"

_Keep this consistent with `config/application_answers.yaml -> why_leaving`.
Positive, forward-looking, two or three sentences, rehearsed._

## Tooling

- Use `/prep-interview {slug}` before each interview to generate a role-specific prep pack and drill the concrete example, decision, outcome habit.
- Use `/log-interview {slug}` immediately after each round to append structured interview notes, set the application stage, and update the feedback log for terminal outcomes.

## Pre-interview checklist

- Pick 6 to 8 stories from the bank for this role and level; write STAR-R bullets for each.
- Rehearse them **out loud**, not in your head.
- For each story, fix one number in memory to land.
- Prepare at least two **decision** stories (a time you chose X over Y and why).
- Pre-map the likely questions for this specific role to specific stories.
- Self-test on three behavioural questions: did I give a concrete example in the first two sentences? did I name a decision and a trade-off? did I land a number?
- Prepare one sharp question about how the role drives the organisation's outcomes.
