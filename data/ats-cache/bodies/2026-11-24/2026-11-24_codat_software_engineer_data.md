<p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>About Codat</strong></p><p style="min-height:1.5em">Codat is an advisory intelligence solution purpose-built for modern commercial banking. Through rich, specialized data, forward-looking insights, and integrated workflows, Codat empowers banking teams to deepen their relationships, grow their revenue, and simplify their day-to-day work.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em">Founded in 2017 and backed by JPMorgan, PayPal, Amex, Plaid, and Shopify, Codat has successfully powered over 350,000 connections to business customers’ financial systems — and is trusted by industry leaders to turn scattered information into actionable, strategic advantages in real time, every time.</p><h2><strong>The Role</strong></h2><p style="min-height:1.5em">Some of the biggest commercial banks in the world already work with us. Their relationship managers want to spend their time with clients, not digging through the books to find out what's changed. We're building the thing that does the digging for them.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em">It's called the Advisory Intelligence Platform. It puts Codat's client intelligence inside the assistant a banker already uses, starting with Microsoft Copilot. Ask it anything about a client and it answers from that client's actual books. Don't ask, and it tells you anyway when something important changes.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em">Every one of those answers sits on data. Someone has to get that data in, shape it, define it and serve it, so the number the assistant quotes is the right one. That's you. You write production software, and you care about the data it moves as much as the code that moves it.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em">This role is for an engineer who's growing fast and wants room to grow faster. You'll ship from your first weeks, own what you build once it's live, and work alongside data and AI engineers who'll push you. Codat is AI-first, not AI bolted onto SaaS, and we work that way ourselves: agents take the repetitive work, engineers take the interesting problems.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em"></p><h2><strong>What You'll Work On</strong></h2><ul style="min-height:1.5em"><li><p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>The data behind every answer.</strong> Services and pipelines that take raw data from accounting platforms, banks and payment systems and turn it into something a model can trust. When a source sends rubbish, your code notices.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>The semantic layer.</strong> The definitions the model is allowed to use: what counts as revenue, when a month ends, which currency. Get it right and every answer agrees with every other answer.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>The signal layer.</strong> Spotting when something changes for a client: a customer paying later, cash running tighter, a supplier quietly becoming critical. You'll help build the detection and make it fast and reliable.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>The tools the assistant calls.</strong> MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers and APIs that serve data to agents. You'll build them, test them and find out how they break before a banker does.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em"><strong>Keeping the numbers honest.</strong> Tests, data checks and observability that prove a number is right, and catch it the moment it isn't.</p></li></ul><h2><strong>You'll Probably Have</strong></h2><ul style="min-height:1.5em"><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Shipped production software that real people depended on, and written most of it yourself. Services, APIs or data systems with tests, not notebooks or config.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Had data be the hard part. A source that sent duplicates, a schema that changed under you, two teams who meant different things by "customer". You fixed it, and you'd design it differently now.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Good engineering habits in Python, C# or another general-purpose language. Tested, modular code the next person can change without fear.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">An eye for SQL. You can read a query and tell whether it's right, whoever or whatever wrote it.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Stayed on the hook for something after it went live. You found out why it broke, fixed the cause, and added what would have caught it sooner.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Some time with CI/CD, Docker and cloud (we're on Azure). You've changed a pipeline, not just pushed to one.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">A habit of asking why. If someone asks for a number the data can't support, you say so.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">AI as part of how you work, and you can explain and defend what it helped you build.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Momentum. More ownership with each move, new stacks picked up quickly, things built that nobody asked for.</p></li><li><p style="min-height:1.5em">Useful, not essential: fintech, lending or accounting; Spark, Databricks or dbt; anything that puts data in front of an LLM.</p></li></ul><p style="min-height:1.5em">Don't tick every box? Apply anyway. We're more interested in what you've built and how fast you're growing than in a perfect match.</p><p style="min-height:1.5em"></p><h2><strong>How We Work</strong></h2><p style="min-height:1.5em">We're a small team with plenty to do. Things move fast: we'd rather ship something useful and improve it than polish it in private.
